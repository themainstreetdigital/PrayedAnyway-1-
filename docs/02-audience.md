# 02 · Audience Profile

## Who we're making this for

**Core viewer:** 18–29, raised around church or church-adjacent culture, currently somewhere between
deconstructing and reconstructing. Spiritually curious, institutionally suspicious.
They'll watch a 12-minute video essay about loneliness at 1am, but they skipped church on Sunday.

**Secondary viewer:** spiritually open Gen Z with no church background, arriving via the aesthetic
(dark academia / "hopecore" / cinematic ambient) rather than via faith keywords.

## What this generation is actually carrying

These are the felt needs every video should map to (pick one per video, name it in the first 30 seconds):

- **Anxiety & burnout** — they were promised hustle would work; it didn't.
- **Loneliness** — most digitally connected, most isolated generation on record.
- **Decision paralysis** — infinite options, terror of choosing wrong ("what is God's will" is really "what if I ruin my life").
- **Shame & comparison** — performing a self online and feeling like a fraud everywhere.
- **Grief & disillusionment** — with institutions, including the church that hurt some of them.
- **Longing for transcendence** — astrology, manifestation, and "the universe" are filling a vacuum. The hunger is real; the address is wrong.

## What earns their trust (and what burns it)

**Earns trust**
- Naming the hard thing first, plainly, without a pitch.
- Showing the Bible's weird, dark, human parts honestly (Lament Psalms, Ecclesiastes, Job, Jonah's sulking).
- "You're not the first person to feel this" — pattern-matching their pain to ancient text.
- Production care. Aesthetic *is* credibility for this audience.
- Open endings. Invitations, not altar calls.

**Burns trust**
- Toxic positivity. "Just trust God" with no acknowledgment of pain.
- Culture-war bait, dunking on other groups, rage-thumbnails.
- Hidden upsell energy — if the video feels like a funnel, they're gone.
- Pretending certainty about things the text holds loosely.

## Viewing behavior (and what it means for us)

- They find long-form via Shorts/TikTok/Reels clips → **every video must yield 3–5 vertical clips** (the cold open, the strike moment, one standalone reframe).
- They watch with captions on, often at night, often on phones → **burned-in stylized captions, mix dialogue loud, design for small dark screens.**
- 8–15 min is the video-essay sweet spot they already binge (think: the quieter side of video-essay YouTube).
- Comments are the community. End every video with a real question worth answering; pin a comment continuing the thought; reply early.

## Search & discovery angles

Title to the felt need, not the doctrine. They search "why do I feel behind in life,"
not "a study of Psalm 13." The biblical concept is the payload; the felt need is the doorway.

| They type / feel | We deliver |
|---|---|
| "why does God feel silent" | The 4 centuries between Malachi and Matthew |
| "I ruined my life" | Peter's denial → breakfast on the beach |
| "everyone's life is better than mine" | Comparison, Saul watching David |
| "I can't rest" | Sabbath as protest |
| "does my anger disqualify me" | Lament Psalms — anger *as* prayer |
