# 03 · Video Formats

Five repeatable faceless formats. Every format follows the channel's master arc —
**dark → honest → strike → hope → open hand** — but each has its own structure and pacing.
Word-count guide: VO at ~130 wpm means a 10-min video ≈ 1,200–1,400 words (silence and music eat the rest).

---

## Format A — "The Midnight Question" (flagship video essay)
**Length:** 10–14 min · **Cadence:** weekly if possible · **Felt need:** anxiety, doubt, shame

A modern Gen Z pain point, traced into a biblical story that's been hiding the answer.

| Beat | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | 0:00–0:40 | The felt need, second person, no intro, no logo. "It's 1:43am and you're awake again." |
| Title strike-in | 0:40–0:55 | Channel mark + episode title, one match-strike frame, music enters |
| Sit in it | 0:55–3:00 | Make the problem vivid and specific. Receipts from modern life. No Bible yet. |
| The pivot line | ~3:00 | "You're not the first." Hard cut to the ancient story. |
| The story, told darkly | 3:00–7:30 | Retell the biblical narrative cinematically, present tense, with its full weirdness intact |
| **The strike** | ~7:30–8:30 | The turn. Match-strike sound, amber enters, the reframe lands in one sentence |
| What this means at 1am | 8:30–11:30 | Walk the reframe back into the viewer's actual life. 2–3 concrete implications |
| Open hand | 11:30–end | One small practice + one honest question for the comments. Soft outro, no hard CTA |

---

## Format B — "Dark Chapters" (the Bible's strangest stories, played straight)
**Length:** 8–12 min · **Felt need:** curiosity, disillusionment ("they never taught me this")

Narrative-first. The weird text IS the hook: Ehud, the witch of Endor, Jacob wrestling, the bronze snake.
Structure: cold-open mid-scene at the strangest moment → rewind ("48 hours earlier...") → full retelling
with whimsy-layer illustrations carrying the visuals → strike: why this story is in the Bible at all →
what it says about a God who keeps weird people. High clip-yield format; the cold open is a ready-made Short.

## Format C — "Words That Got Lost" (one word, 8–10 min)
**Length:** 8–10 min · **Felt need:** longing for depth, language for feelings

One Hebrew/Greek word per episode — *hesed, selah, ruach, shalom, teshuvah, makrothymia*.
Structure: a modern moment where English fails us → the word, drawn on screen letter by letter
(signature whimsy visual) → 3 scenes from scripture where the word appears, escalating →
strike: the meaning English flattened → carry the word into one ordinary Tuesday moment.
Most binge-able and most playlist-able format; lowest production cost.

## Format D — "Prayers of the Exhausted" (lament + ambience)
**Length:** 9–15 min · **Felt need:** burnout, grief, insomnia · **Use:** the channel's "night mode"

Slow walk through a lament Psalm (13, 42, 88...) — part essay, part guided stillness.
Long ambient passages, rain, candle close-ups, scripture text appearing line by line.
Less narration per minute than any other format; designed to be watched falling asleep,
which is a feature (rewatches, "this playlist got me through" comments). Psalm 88 episode ends dark
on purpose — the one video with no strike — and says why.

## Format E — "The Anyway Files" (testimony-shaped history)
**Length:** 10–13 min · **Felt need:** hopelessness, "is this real for anyone"

People who prayed anyway — biblical and historical: Hannah, Habakkuk, Corrie ten Boom,
the slave-church spirituals, modern anonymized stories (with permission). Documentary cadence:
archival textures, paper photographs with hand-drawn amber halos, dates typed on screen.
The strike beat is always a real quote from the person.

## Clip strategy (applies to all formats)

From every long video, cut before publishing:
1. **The cold open** (0:00–0:40) as a vertical Short ending on the question → "full story on the channel."
2. **The strike moment** with 15s of lead-in.
3. **One standalone reframe line** over the best single shot, captioned large.

## Publishing rhythm (sustainable solo pace)

- 1 long video / week: rotate A → B → C → A → D → B... (Format A appears most).
- 3 Shorts / week, cut from the long videos (no extra writing).
- Format D episodes can be batched — 3 in one production day — and held as schedule insurance.
